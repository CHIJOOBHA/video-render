# STORY RULES (how each section is told; the LOCKED rules and the notes always win — never skip, change or add a notes point to fit these)

1. SHOW FIRST, THEN EXPLAIN. Open every section with something concrete on screen (an object, a document line, a map
   detail, a number, a drawing) and let the first beat ask about it. The meaning comes a beat or two later. Never open a
   section with the bare definition or thesis sentence; say it right after the visual has made the viewer curious.

2. NO ABSTRACT TALK WITHOUT A PICTURE. No more than about 10 seconds (roughly 25 spoken words) of abstract explanation
   without a concrete on-screen anchor for it. When an idea is abstract, give it an object, example, diagram or short
   story beat to hang on. Each drawing must belong to what is being said at that moment.

3. EVERY SECTION IS A SMALL DETECTIVE STORY (30–90 s). Follow this loop, in this order:
   notice (the visual) → question (what is this / why?) → partial answer → complication (a doubt, exception or "but…") →
   full answer (the complete notes point, every item spoken). Join sections with a one-line bridge that carries the
   viewer forward ("So that explains X — but then, why Y?") so the part never feels cut in the middle.

Short notes points may use a shorter loop (notice → question → full answer); the full answer is never skipped or shortened.
